Lecture 8 — Data Visualization and Interpretation

Data visualization is an essential step in data analysis. It helps summarize data, identify patterns, detect outliers, and communicate results effectively.

1. Importance of Data Visualization

  • Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) to understand trends and distribution.
  • Detect anomalies or missing data.
  • Compare groups or variables easily.
  • Communicate findings in research papers or presentations.
Example: Plotting blood glucose levels across a patient group can highlight abnormal values and variability.

2. Types of Graphs and Plots

a) Bar Plot

Used for categorical data to compare counts or percentages.

barplot(table(df$BloodType), col="lightgreen", main="Blood Type Distribution")
Count of blood types (A, B, AB, O) in a sample of 200 patients.

b) Histogram

Shows frequency distribution of continuous variables.

hist(df$Glucose, breaks=10, col="lightblue", main="Histogram of Glucose Levels", xlab="Glucose (mg/dL)")
Visualize distribution of fasting glucose levels in diabetic patients.

c) Box Plot

Displays median, quartiles, and outliers.

boxplot(df$Cholesterol ~ df$Gender, col=c("pink","lightblue"), main="Cholesterol by Gender")
Compare cholesterol levels between males and females, identify outliers.

d) Scatter Plot

Used to observe relationship between two continuous variables.

plot(df$Weight, df$Glucose, xlab="Weight (kg)", ylab="Glucose (mg/dL)", main="Glucose vs Weight")
Detect trends or correlations between patient weight and blood glucose levels.

e) Line Plot

Useful for time-series data.

plot(df$Time, df$Glucose, type="l", col="red", main="Glucose over Time", xlab="Time (hours)", ylab="Glucose")
Track glucose levels of a patient over 24 hours to monitor fluctuations.

f) Pie Chart

Shows proportion of categories as slices of a circle.

pie(table(df$Medication), col=rainbow(length(unique(df$Medication))), main="Medication Usage")
Percentage of patients taking different types of medications.

3. Interpretation of Plots

  • Look at central tendency and spread (mean, median, variance) from histograms and box plots.
  • Identify relationships or correlations in scatter plots.
  • Detect outliers, missing values, or unusual patterns.
  • Use color, labels, and legends for clarity.

4. Summary

  • Data visualization is essential for understanding, summarizing, and communicating data.
  • Different types of plots are suitable for different types of data (categorical vs continuous, time-series vs grouped data).
  • Proper interpretation allows identification of trends, anomalies, and relationships.
  • Visualization is a critical skill in biological, clinical, and research-based datasets.
